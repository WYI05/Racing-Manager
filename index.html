Three facts define every strategic choice in this race:

1. Karts rotate every stint. Nobody keeps a good kart and nobody is stuck with a bad one. Machinery averages out over 9 stints, so the race is decided by driver consistency, clean pit rotations, and traffic management — not by equipment or a hero lap.

2. Stints are fixed 20-minute blocks. There is no fuel window to play with and no undercut. "Strategy" collapses into three levers you fully control: who drives which stints, how flawlessly you execute 8 rotations, and how you feed information to the driver with no radio.

3. It's an evening race. Sunset in Las Vegas on Sep 5 is around 7:04 PM — the green flag drops into the sunset. Stint 1 is driven in low-sun glare, stints 2–3 in fading twilight, stints 4–9 fully under floodlights. Whoever starts needs a plan for glare (tear-offs / tinted visor for stint 1 only, clear after); expect grip to improve as the track cools.

Stint planner

Tap a stint to swap driver

Nine stints, two drivers: someone drives five. Give the five to whichever of you is more consistent over 20 minutes (not whoever has the best single lap), and give that same driver the last stint — stint 9 is where tired teams throw it away. This planner saves on this device, so set it now and pull it up on race day.

Default recommendation: pure alternation. You swap karts every stint anyway, so a driver change costs you nothing extra — and alternating caps every rest gap at 20 minutes, which in Vegas heat is worth more than any theoretical gain from double-stinting. Only double-stint if the briefing reveals that driver changes cost extra time versus a same-driver kart swap.
Confirm at briefing: may the same driver take consecutive stints at all? Some SWS tracks mandate a driver change at every rotation or set a max drive time per driver. If changes are mandatory every stint, alternation is forced and the only decision left is who starts.

Qualifying plan

6:00 PM · random kart

Don't over-invest here. Your qualifying kart is randomly assigned and you hand it back before the race — a mega lap in a fast kart or a doomed lap in a slow one, either way it only buys grid position for a 3-hour race with 8 rotations. Worth having, not worth risk.

The plan that follows from that:

Faster qualifier goes second in the session — track is slightly cooler and more rubbered every minute after 6 PM. Use the first runner's laps as the scout report: kart feel, where the grip is, traffic patterns.

Hunt clean air, not the perfect lap. With up to 16 teams sharing one track, a traffic-free launch matters more than tenths. Build one out-lap gap, do 2–3 push laps, and bank it.

Whoever qualifies does not have to start. Confirm at briefing whether the starting driver is free choice — if it is, you can give quali to your one-lap driver and the glare-heavy first stint to whoever handles low sun better.

Rotations — the 8 moments that decide the race

Kart swap every stint

With equal machinery and fixed stints, almost all recoverable time lives in the 8 rotations. A rehearsed changeover beats a fast lap every time. Until the briefing defines the exact procedure, build around this default choreography:

T–5:00Incoming driver: last drink done, helmet and gloves on, balaclava set. Stop watching the timing screen.
T–3:00Incoming driver walks to the swap zone. Pit board goes up for the driver on track: 3 MIN
T–1:00Board: IN NOW (or as the track's rotation signal dictates). Incoming driver at the assigned kart lane.
T–0:00Swap. Outgoing driver carries the ballast handoff if ballast is per-team, confirms belts/seat insert for the new kart, points out any kart issue to staff immediately — before the new stint starts, not after.
T+2:00Outgoing driver drinks first, then debriefs to the board (kart feel, track spots), then rests. Ten of your twenty rest minutes are for recovery, not spectating.
Kart handover intel is your only scouting tool. You inherit a different kart every stint — likely one another team just ran. Agree a 5-second hand signal for "this kart is bad" so the incoming driver knows to adapt the first lap, not discover it in turn 1.

No radios: the signal book

Agree these before Saturday

Radios are banned at your track, so the resting driver is the pit wall. Fix one board position on the fence (pick it at the 6:45 grid walk, somewhere on a straight with time to read), show the board for 2 consecutive laps minimum, and keep the vocabulary this small:

BoardMeansNotes
P4Current positionShow every 3–4 laps by default
+2.5 / –4.0Gap to kart ahead / behindOnly when a fight is live
PUSHRival in reach — go nowUse sparingly or it means nothing
EASYHold position, save energyBig gap both ways, or last laps of a stint
5 MIN / 3 MINStint time remainingDriver has no clock — this is essential
IN NOWRotation this lapFollow the track's own signal if they run one

Driver-to-pit hand signals (agree these too): raised fist = kart problem, point at rear = engine/brakes, thumbs-up on the straight = all fine, tapping helmet = can't read the board / show again.

Blind window: in the last ~3 minutes of every stint the board is unmanned — the resting driver is walking to the swap zone. Show position and time before T–3:00; the driver on track should assume no new information after that.

Ballast, heat, and the human machine

Vegas in September

Ballast is confirmed but the target weight is not published. Call the track and get the equalization number and both of your weights into the plan. If ballast brings you to a fixed combined figure, weight difference between you two stops mattering; if it's stepped (weight classes), know which class each of you lands in before Saturday. Weigh in wearing what you'll drive in.

Heat is a real opponent. Early-September Las Vegas: ~100°F daytime, still ~90°F at the 7 PM green flag, cooling through the race. Between stints: shade, electrolytes (not just water), helmet off, sit down. Salty snacks between stints, real meal before 4 PM, nothing heavy after. The driver doing 5 stints sweats through 100 minutes of racing — hydrate starting Friday, not Saturday afternoon.

Hands and ribs give out before lungs do. 100 minutes of rental-kart steering without power assistance: bring proper karting gloves (two pairs if you have them — dry pair mid-race feels incredible) and consider a rib protector; the RT10 seat plus 8 different karts' worth of seat fit is a rib bruise generator.

Eligibility floor per the event sheet: 15 years old and 120 lbs minimum — both of you clear it, but bring ID; the track can ask for identity proof against your SWS licence.

What this race is worth (SWS)

Endurance Cup 2026

Scoring is field-size driven: the winner takes 1,000 + number of SWS teams (so ~1,008–1,016 pts here), last place takes just the participation floor, a logarithmic curve in between — plus +10 bonus points to every team for the 3-hour duration. Your season ranking sums your team's 12 best results.

Thinking about SWS International Finals? A 2-driver team can race and score all year but cannot qualify for the Finals — qualification requires 3–4 drivers with at least 3 of them having driven 3+ times with the team in 2026. If that's an ambition, recruit driver #3 now: between Aug 1 and Dec 31 you can only add drivers to the team (additions are irreversible, no deletions), and they'd need 3 races before New Year's Eve. The event needs 6+ SWS teams to count for points at all — another reason the 8-team bubble matters.

Also in the rulebook: a team may only run one endurance event per day, a driver can belong to max 3 teams, and results go definitive 10 days after upload — check yours promptly and raise any complaint through the track (SWS won't take it directly from you).

Unknowns the SWS rulebook doesn't cover

The SWS regulation deliberately leaves race procedure to the organizer. Everything below is track-defined and strategy-critical — get answers before Saturday, or at the 5:30 briefing at the latest:

  1. Are driver changes mandatory at every rotation, and is there a max drive time per driver?Decides whether double-stinting is even legal — the whole planner hinges on this.
  2. What exactly happens at a rotation?Race suspended and everyone swaps at once, or rolling pit windows? Is the 20 min on-track time or including the swap? How is the running order preserved through the swap?
  3. What is the ballast equalization target and increment?And is it weighed per driver per stint, or fixed per seat?
  4. How does the 30-min qualifying split across a 2-driver team, and must both drivers qualify?The "10 min per driver" note assumes 3 drivers.
  5. Can we choose our starting driver freely?Or must the qualifier start?
  6. What's the penalty system?Contact, jump start, pit lane conduct — and is there a kart-swap procedure penalty (e.g., belts done by staff only)?
  7. Is a pit board allowed at the fence, and where?Some tracks restrict where spectators/teammates can signal from. Also: any live timing screen visible from the pit?
  8. What happens if a kart fails mid-stint?Swap kart with laps credited, or tough luck? Changes how hard you push a suspect kart.
